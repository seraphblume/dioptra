# v0.3 design notes

Status: **partly implemented in shadow mode.** Layers 1–2, the monocular candidate and the flags marked *implemented* below now run in `js/engine-v03.js` alongside the frozen v0.2 predictor. See the [v0.3 engine specification](v0.3-engine.md). Layers 4–5 remain design.

## Proposed architecture

```text
raw observations
  -> normalization and validation
  -> evidence-quality / disagreement layer
  -> monocular refractive candidate
  -> binocular consistency layer
  -> subjective-endpoint estimate
  -> optional prescribing/adaptation layer
  -> prediction + flags + explanation + provenance
```

The spectacle refraction engine ends before contact-lens conversion, progressive fitting or product recommendation.

## Layer 1: preserve and normalize observations

- Keep the original S/C/A value and method.
- Normalize axis to 1–180 without losing the raw value.
- Convert a copy to `M`, `J0`, `J45` for analysis.
- Keep every autorefractor repeat and the machine-selected line.
- Treat undocumented instrument quality tokens as opaque.
- Stamp instrument, collection conditions and missingness.
- Reject impossible types/ranges at entry; flag unusual but possible values for review instead of silently clamping them.

## Layer 2: evidence quality and agreement

Candidate evidence channels are habitual Rx, repeated AR, retinoscopy and keratometry. They must not be treated as interchangeable.

For repeated AR, v0.3 computes a component-wise vector median, the range of M, the J0/J45 spread and the selected-line distance (implemented). For cross-source comparisons, compute pairwise vector deltas and preserve which methods disagree. Do not choose clinical thresholds until enough clean data exists.

Suggested outputs:

- `measurement_quality`: completeness and repeatability;
- `objective_agreement`: agreement among available objective channels;
- `clinical_completeness`: whether the expected screen and endpoint fields exist;
- `population_support`: whether the case resembles the development/validation population;
- `overall_status`: `insufficient_evidence`, `review`, or `supported`.

`overall_status` is not a calibrated probability. A numeric probability should be shown only after calibration on held-out prospective data.

## Layer 3: monocular candidate

Train or fit the monocular refractive model against the **subjective endpoint**, not against the dispensed Rx. Inputs should be introduced in planned increments:

1. selected AR S/C/A baseline;
2. repeatability features;
3. habitual Rx and corrected VA;
4. retinoscopy;
5. keratometry as an independent corneal observation;
6. permitted history/VA context.

Each addition needs an ablation analysis and prospective validation. Preliminary health tests remain flags unless a preregistered study shows a reliable predictive role.

## Layer 4: binocular consistency

After monocular candidates are created:

- compare interocular spherical-equivalent and vector differences with habitual balance;
- use recorded best VA and binocular-balance eligibility;
- retain dominant-eye context only when measured;
- flag large new anisometropic or astigmatic changes for review;
- do not force symmetry.

Binocular balance is conventionally done only when VA is equal. If the condition is absent, record that the procedure was not eligible rather than fabricating a result.

## Layer 5: prescribing/adaptation

If there is enough data, a separate model may estimate the **issued prescription** from the subjective endpoint plus habitual adaptation, visual task, ambulatory response, binocular comfort and clinician-coded reason. This layer must never be used when evaluating the optical endpoint model.

The UI should always show:

- estimated subjective endpoint;
- any proposed prescribing adjustment;
- the reason and evidence for that adjustment;
- the unmodified source measurements.

## Candidate flags

Names are stable concepts. Thresholds marked **TBD** must be learned or clinically approved. Flags marked *implemented* run in v0.3 shadow mode with provisional thresholds from `js/config-v03.js`; they only raise review status.

| Flag | Meaning | Initial action |
|---|---|---|
| `AR_INCOMPLETE` | Selected or required per-eye AR data missing. | Abstain. |
| `AR_REPEATABILITY_LOW` | Repeat readings have high vector dispersion (*implemented*, provisional 0.50 D range of M; plus `AR_ASTIGMATISM_UNSTABLE` for J0/J45). | Lower evidence status; retain all readings. |
| `AR_SELECTED_OUTSIDE_CLUSTER` | Machine-selected line differs materially from robust center (*implemented*, provisional 0.25 D). | Review selected line. |
| `OBJECTIVE_DISAGREEMENT` | AR, retinoscopy, habitual Rx or keratometry disagree beyond method-specific limits (TBD; keratometry part *implemented* as `KERATOMETRY_DISAGREEMENT`). | Show pairwise disagreement; do not silently average. |
| `HABITUAL_CHANGE_LARGE` | Candidate differs materially from habitual correction (TBD). | Require adaptation review and explicit prescribing reason if modified. |
| `PINHOLE_LIMITED_GAIN` | Reduced VA shows limited/absent recorded pinhole improvement. | Flag that refraction alone may not explain VA; no diagnosis. |
| `ENDPOINT_BELOW_RECORDED_PINHOLE` | Best-corrected endpoint VA is worse than the recorded pinhole result. | Check data/test conditions and review. |
| `DUOCHROME_UNUSABLE` | Colour-vision status or exam conditions make duochrome unavailable/unreliable. | Record alternate sphere-refinement method. |
| `BINOCULAR_BALANCE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` | Monocular best VA was unequal or the procedure was not possible. | Skip balance and retain reason. |
| `PRELIMINARY_SCREEN_ABNORMAL` | A health, neurologic, binocular or field screen was recorded abnormal. | Clinician review; never a dioptric adjustment. |
| `SUBJECTIVE_TO_PRESCRIBED_CHANGE` | Issued Rx differs from subjective endpoint. | Require reason code and note. |
| `SE_SHIFT_REVIEW` | Combined proposed S/C changes exceed the active SE budget. | Apply the versioned guardrail or abstain; show requested and final shifts. |
| `OUT_OF_DISTRIBUTION` | Case lies outside the validated age/power/instrument/population envelope. | Abstain or label unsupported. |

Flags are not diagnoses and do not replace referral or clinical judgment.

## Safeguards

### Data and provenance

- Immutable algorithm version on lock.
- Immutable raw measurements in the saved record.
- Derived features carry a rule-set version and can be recomputed.
- No direct identifiers in research exports.
- Distinguish missing, not indicated, not performed, unavailable and untestable.

### Prediction

- Preserve minus-cylinder convention or explicitly transpose before comparison.
- Validate axis as circular data.
- Evaluate power changes in S/C/A and vector space.
- Use SE as a combined-power monitor, not as proof that two prescriptions are equivalent.
- Never turn a preliminary-test flag into a dioptric rule without prospective evidence.
- Abstain rather than manufacture a confident value from incomplete or unsupported inputs.

### Study integrity

- Keep v0.2.0 and Cohort B frozen.
- Close and export Cohort B before selecting v0.3 thresholds.
- Separate development, calibration and prospective validation data.
- Group repeat visits from the same patient to prevent train/test leakage.
- Predefine primary outcomes and tolerance metrics.
- Report raw-AR and habitual-Rx baselines alongside any model.
- Report performance by eye and by patient, with confidence intervals and clinically relevant subgroups only when sample size permits.

## Evaluation targets

Retain the current component metrics and add:

- sphere and cylinder bias/MAE;
- circular axis error, stratified by cylinder magnitude;
- spherical-equivalent bias/MAE;
- vector error in `M`, `J0`, `J45`;
- complete-eye and complete-patient tolerance rates;
- calibration/coverage for any confidence or abstention system;
- error at the subjective-endpoint layer separately from error introduced by the prescribing layer;
- comparison with raw AR, habitual Rx and simple frozen heuristics.

## Decisions intentionally deferred

- number of AR repeats required;
- which centre estimator (selected, median, most plus) drives the prediction (all three are now recorded);
- disagreement and out-of-distribution thresholds;
- confidence score formula;
- whether keratometry improves spectacle-Rx prediction;
- whether preliminary findings belong in the predictor at all;
- asymmetric ADD modeling;
- any contact-lens or product-selection module.

Deferral is deliberate: clinical practice justifies collecting these observations, but only study data can show whether they improve prediction.
